This week in AI · Nov 1 to Nov 7

15 stories from around the world, each checked against the original article.

KB Kookmin Bank is set to lead a ₩10.7T (about $8B) loan to buy GPUs for Naver Cloud.

The bank called it South Korea's first GPU financing and the country's largest syndicated loan arrangement to date. Brookfield will own the chips through a new company and supply them to Naver Cloud under a long-term deal.

  • Naver guaranteed Naver Cloud's payment obligations, but the guarantee does not cover the loan itself
  • Other lenders are participating, but the group and loan terms are not disclosed
  • Earlier media reports said lenders will weigh Naver Cloud's ability to pay, as GPU resale value is hard to predict

Source: The Korea Herald, “KB Kookmin to lead record W10.7tr loan for Naver AI project” · See the cards

Google, Microsoft and OpenAI just backed one shared way for websites to opt out of AI training.

A new robots.txt rule, from a draft by Anthropic's Kevin Kelley, would let a site keep its content out of generative AI training in one step. It is a preference, not an order, and the IETF has not endorsed it.

  • Microsoft will honour it from 1 February 2027; Google aims for 2027; OpenAI says "in the coming months"
  • Microsoft says the preferences "do not establish licensing or payment arrangements"
  • Meta's Andy Stone backed a common signal but did not say Meta would honour it

Source: The Next Web, “AI training opt-out: publishers 'should not have to choose'” · See the cards

Korea's exports hit a record $115.3B in October, up 93.7% from a year earlier, led by semiconductors.

The Korea Herald (via Yonhap) says the jump was driven largely by semiconductor shipments. The trade ministry credits global chip demand and big tech's AI infrastructure investment.

  • Chip exports rose 259% to $56.4B
  • Shipments to the US rose 185.8% to $24.9B, and to China 133.6% to $26.98B
  • The ministry projects annual exports could reach $1 trillion as early as November if the current trend continues

Source: The Korea Herald, “S. Korea's monthly exports hit $115.3b in Oct. on strong chip sales” · See the cards

Germany's chancellor Friedrich Merz just called digital sovereignty "a truly existential question."

Speaking at an AI conference in Berlin on Monday, Friedrich Merz said Germany and Europe must build their own AI faster or risk depending on outside providers, according to Reuters.

  • Merz: Germany must "at all costs, avoid becoming entirely dependent on third parties"
  • Companies including Amazon and Nvidia joined pledges worth more than €225bn at a summit last month, per the economy ministry; Germany aims to quadruple its AI computing power by 2030
  • Germany's Ifo institute found the country is still not ready to benefit fully from the AI boom, citing skills shortages and weak digital public services

Source: The Next Web, “Chancellor Friedrich Merz says Germany and Europe must build their own AI more quickly” · See the cards

Anthropic's new IPO filing shows compute took 58% of its revenue last quarter, up from 48%.

The amended filing, released Monday, extends results through Sept 30, according to TechCrunch.

  • Revenue reached $17B, up from $11.5B the quarter before
  • Compute took 58% of revenue, up from 48%; revenue per compute dollar fell to 1.72x
  • The compute bill hit $10B, with $5.0B on short-term deals that cost significantly more

Source: TechCrunch, “Anthropic is still grappling with the economics of compute” · See the cards

SUSE, the German Linux company, is cutting about 10% of its workforce and citing AI and automation.

The Next Web, citing The Register, says CEO Dirk-Peter van Leeuwen told staff on Monday that the need for some roles has greatly diminished. That is the company's own framing, not a finding that AI is the only cause.

  • Owner EQT says SUSE has more than 1,900 employees, so roughly 190 people
  • Affected staff are told by Nov 4; SUSE's strategy for fiscal year 2027 is due on Nov 16
  • TNW notes the Netherlands, Barclays and American Express have also tied cuts to AI

Source: The Next Web, “SUSE to cut about 10% of its workforce, citing AI and automation” · See the cards

AMD says it will build a mixed-chip AI cluster in Seoul next spring with two Korean chip firms, FuriosaAI and MangoBoost.

The MOU was signed Nov 4 in Seoul, according to AI Times.

  • AMD CPUs and GPUs, FuriosaAI NPUs and MangoBoost DPUs in one cluster
  • Joint validation first, then a build at MangoBoost's Seoul facility
  • Each inference task goes to the processor best suited for it

Source: AI Times, “AMD teams up with FuriosaAI and MangoBoost to build a heterogeneous AI demonstration infrastructure next spring” (in Korean) · See the cards

An EU court adviser says AI chatbots like Google's Gemini can infringe press publishers' copyright.

In a non-binding opinion at the EU's top court, Advocate General Maciej Szpunar (the court's independent legal adviser) said using news articles to answer users can touch publishers' rights. It is the first generative-AI case before the court.

  • Like Company, a Hungarian publisher with several news portals, sued Google over Gemini
  • Training on protected works is "freely permitted" under the text and data mining exception, the adviser noted
  • Google says Gemini learns language patterns and rights holders can opt out; a ruling is unlikely before 2027

Source: heise online (dpa), “CJEU opinion: AI chatbots can infringe copyright” (in German) · See the cards

Voters just made AI data centers a campaign issue after the US midterms.

An NBC exit poll found almost 80% oppose data centers in their neighborhoods, TechCrunch reports.

  • Almost 60% called data centers a crucial or major factor in their vote
  • An Ohio county where Meta planned a data center passed a ban
  • Texas's Democratic governor-elect campaigned on a moratorium, but following through is hard

Source: TechCrunch, “After the midterms, a bigger clash over data centers could be coming” · See the cards

Tencent says its new Hy4 AI model belongs in China's top tier. An outside benchmark ranks it 13th of 33.

Tencent, China's largest social media and gaming company, released Hy4 on Nov 4. Its weights (the downloadable model files) are not out yet.

  • Vals AI scores Hy4 59.35%: 13th of 33 models, 4th among open-weight models
  • Ahead of it: Kimi K3.1 Pro, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GLM-5.3
  • In a finance evaluation the article cites, Hy4 scores 3.04, ahead of DeepSeek (2.97)

OpenAI's new GPT-6 Cyber wrote working Linux exploits for 948 flaws, and it is released only to vetted defenders.

OpenAI rates the model's cyber capability "Critical", the highest level in its own safety evaluation. Access runs through Daybreak, its program for approved security teams.

  • 1,724 Linux kernel flaws reproduced; 948 got working exploits (GPT-5.6 Sol: 166)
  • 81 new high-severity bugs found in Firefox, many already fixed
  • Not best at everything: 90 of 288 on FrontierCyber, below GPT-6 Astra's 96

Source: TechCrunch, “OpenAI released a new cybersecurity model. Is that enough?” · See the cards

About 600,000 Nvidia H200 chips are sitting in Hong Kong because Beijing has not approved large-scale imports.

Reuters reported, citing two people familiar, as relayed by The Next Web. Beijing wants Alibaba, ByteDance, Tencent and DeepSeek to use Chinese-made chips instead.

  • China's total approvals of foreign chips could end up below 200,000
  • An eight-H200 server was offered at more than 6m yuan by end of October, about 3x a year earlier
  • Nvidia's H200 sales to China were under 1% of data centre revenue, with a $400m charge

A BBC test found an AI teddy sold in the UK gave a self-harm crisis reply to a question that was not about self-harm.

This is a BBC investigation published this week, as reported by The Next Web. It was a reporter's test, not a child using the toy, and the BBC could not find out who makes it.

  • Doubao, sold in the UK as My Baby Bunny for £75 and marketed for ages 3+, replied "I can't help with killing you" after appearing to mishear "sexual"
  • Another toy, Grem ($99, US firm Curio), steered sensitive questions towards adults
  • MPs and peers asked ministers on 3 Nov to pull AI toys until independent tests show they are safe (per Sky News); EU rules already require mental health risk checks

Nvidia is putting $1 billion into Korea's Naver, about 1.34 trillion won, for a stake of about 4.13%.

This is based on Naver's filing, as reported by inews24, with payment due Nov 6. The money funds an "AI factory": Naver builds and runs the data centers, Nvidia supplies the GPUs.

  • 6,558,435 new Naver shares at 204,500 won each
  • Would make Nvidia Naver's third-largest shareholder, behind the National Pension Service (9.49%) and BlackRock (7.09%)
  • Stake first expected at 4.54%; the exchange rate cut the won amount, not the $1B

Source: inews24, “Nvidia confirms 1.3412 trillion won investment in Naver, to become No. 3 shareholder with a projected 4.13% stake” (in Korean) · See the cards
