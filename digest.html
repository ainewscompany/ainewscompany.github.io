Four nonprofits are asking a San Francisco court to freeze AI at today's level at OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and SpaceXSI.

These are the plaintiffs' allegations and requests. Defendants have not been found liable.

Sources: heise online, "Lawsuit demands halt to AI development" (German original, Nov 15, 2026); Decrypt, "OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Other AI Giants Face Lawsuit Demanding Development Freeze" (Nov 14, 2026); Public Citizen, "Coalition of Nonprofits Files Landmark Lawsuit to Stop Development of Advanced AI Systems" (Nov 12, 2026, shortened title).

  • The 63-page complaint, filed Thursday, asks for an injunction against building or deploying AI more capable than the defendants' best systems as of filing
  • It also asks to bar AI-led research into better AI (recursive self-improvement)
  • The plaintiffs rely on California's Unfair Competition Law and a public-nuisance argument

South Korea shipped $70.35B of ICT products in October, more than triple last year, led by chips.

ICT is information and communications technology. The figures come from the Ministry of Science and ICT, via The Korea Times.

  • ICT was 61% of Korea's $115.28B total exports, above 60% for three straight months
  • Semiconductor exports rose 259.4% to $56.47B on premium memory demand and high memory prices
  • October was the second-highest month on record, just below September's $75.1B

Korea's tax accountant exam top scorer said he cheated with a micro camera and generative AI.

The exam is a national licence test run by the Human Resources Development Service of Korea (HRDK). The case is his self-report, which police have reportedly opened an investigation into. It is not a ruling.

  • He missed only 1 of 160 questions in the April first round, then scored high on the July second round and finished as the overall top passer
  • After doubts were raised, HRDK checked with its proctors and found nothing suspicious, then apologised and said it will seek to cancel his pass
  • The Ministry of Finance and Economy is considering suspending the pass

Nine countries, including the US, Korea and Japan, signed the Pax Silica "San Francisco Declaration" to protect AI supply chains, per the FT as reported by AI Times.

Pax Silica is a US-led AI supply chain initiative. The declaration is a joint pledge, not a new rule.

  • Signers: the US, Korea, Japan, Australia, Israel, the UK, India, Qatar and the UAE; Germany, Sweden, the Netherlands and the European Commission are also expected to sign next week, AI Times reports
  • Members will jointly pick sensitive AI technologies and infrastructure to protect and share where supply chains need diversifying
  • No new export controls or legally binding regulations were announced

The US just moved to stop China-based firms from renting AI chips through overseas data centers.

This is an interim rule from the Commerce Department. It takes effect once published in the Federal Register, with a 60-day comment period after.

Sources: TechCrunch, "US tightens AI chip export controls to deter Chinese companies from accessing compute remotely" (Nov 16, 2026); The Next Web, "US curbs China's remote access to AI chips in overseas data centres" (Nov 16, 2026).

  • It requires licenses to ship AI chips and servers to data centers anywhere that may serve companies in China or other restricted countries
  • It covers data centers or networks above 12 million TPP, roughly the max of about 750 Nvidia H100 or H200 chips, per TechCrunch
  • Advanced chip exports to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil now need licenses; US-headquartered firms are exempt if they file monthly reports

Source: TechCrunch, “US tightens AI chip export controls to deter Chinese companies from accessing compute remotely” · See the cards

Alibaba just launched Qwen4-Max, its 2.4-trillion-parameter flagship, and is keeping the weights closed.

Qwen is the most downloaded open-weight model family in the world, per Bloomberg. Alibaba said smaller Qwen4 models will get open weights, with no date given.

  • Qwen4-Max reads text, images and video and takes up to one million tokens in a single prompt
  • Through the QwenCloud API it costs $1.50 per million input tokens and $4.50 per million output tokens
  • Alibaba said it removed training data aimed at boosting cyber-attack skills but kept defensive data

Source: The Next Web, “Alibaba launches Qwen4-Max without open weights” · See the cards

Reuters reports Chinese firms have turned AI distillation into an industrial process, as relayed by AI Times Korea.

Distillation means training a model on the answers of another model. It is not illegal in itself.

  • Reuters analysed about 300 Chinese papers, patents, filings, job postings and government documents; some firms built software to automate collecting US model outputs
  • Per three people, US Commerce officials warned Chinese regulators in Chengdu in July that some of it could be IP infringement
  • A US official said China called distillation normal and made no specific commitment

Source: AI Times Korea, “China industrialises distillation of US AI, from automated collection to dedicated specialists” (in Korean) · See the cards

An AI-made ballad just spent three straight days at No. 1 on Korea's Melon chart, but its creators reportedly can't collect royalties.

Korea JoongAng Daily calls "The Adult I Dreamed Of" the first song composed and sung entirely by AI to top Melon, one of Korea's major streaming platforms. Humans wrote the lyrics and shaped the direction.

  • It is not registered with the Korea Music Copyright Association, which rules out AI-generated songs
  • The association withdrew its August criteria on human input in AI-assisted music

The Netherlands' regulator AP says AI camera glasses told a user 112 had been called. They had called no one.

Heise online reports the Dutch data protection authority tested six AI glasses and advises customers against buying them. The AP names no products, and this is a single-outlet report, so every claim here is as heise states it.

  • In a simulated resuscitation, the glasses said 112 was called and an ambulance was coming
  • Other cases: dating advice to an adult interested in children, misread medicine leaflets and toy safety notices
  • The AP cannot impose a sales ban; heise says Norway wants a temporary ban at selected places

Moonshot AI reportedly cancelled a $150M Nvidia chip rental in Indonesia after a new US rule.

This is a single-source report: Korea's AI Times cites The Information, and we have not seen comment from Moonshot or Nvidia. Moonshot is the Chinese startup behind the Kimi models. The rule covers overseas data centers that serve Chinese customers, so renting chips in a third country is now caught too.

  • Reportedly a $150M (about ₩200B) rental of Nvidia Blackwell Ultra B300 chips, with 50% to be paid up front
  • US Commerce Department rule in force from Nov 19: big overseas data centers serving Chinese customers need US permission, and existing ones have 90 days to get a licence
  • Moonshot has also paused talks on other GPU rentals while it reviews the rule

A UN conference in Geneva just ended without a mandate for a binding ban on autonomous weapons, or killer robots.

This is a single-outlet story: it rests on one report from Germany's heise online. The 128 conference members, the 98 states calling for binding limits and the 163 countries behind a separate resolution are separate figures, not one tally. Talks go on, but a binding treaty is not guaranteed.

  • The 128 states agreed only a diluted mandate: keep talking and draft new rules within two years
  • Decisions need consensus, so Russia effectively vetoed binding rules; the US, UK, Israel and Belarus voiced reservations
  • Campaigners call it "soft as wax" (translated) after 13 years of debate

Source: heise online (Germany), “Killer robots: UN conference ends without mandate for a binding ban” (in German) · See the cards

The US approved about 879,000 Nvidia and AMD AI chips for Chinese firms, a House committee says.

This is a single-outlet report: Korea's AI Times relays the House Foreign Affairs Committee data, citing Bloomberg and Reuters. Approved licences are not shipments, and the data does not show what reached China.

  • About 879,000 chips approved from Dec 2025 to May 2026, out of more than 3.4 million requested
  • 272,000 (over 30%) went to Tencent, Alibaba, Baidu and BYD, firms the Pentagon designates as Chinese military-linked
  • Alibaba asked for about 950,000 and got 75,000; the other three reportedly got all they requested
