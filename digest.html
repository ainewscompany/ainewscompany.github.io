This week in AI · Oct 18 to Oct 24

8 stories from around the world, each checked against the original article.

The rocket company's biggest business is now AI.

SpaceX's third-quarter filing shows $14.6B in revenue, and TechCrunch says AI now makes up 52% of its "income" (its term; AI profit was $431M).

  • The renamed "Super Intelligence" segment brought in $7.5B in revenue (company revenue also got about $1B from the recovery of Bitcoin prices and related-party income)
  • Q3 capital expenditures were $19.6B, with $35B to $40B of new investment expected in Q4
  • Musk predicts SpaceX's annual recurring revenue will be $500B to $1T by the end of 2027 (his forecast, not a result)

Source: TechCrunch, “SpaceX's AI business is beating expectations” · See the cards

One government just told its agencies to drop a Chinese AI model.

The US ordered federal civilian agencies to stop buying DeepSeek, a Chinese AI startup, and remove it from government IT systems, according to the Department of Homeland Security (DHS).

  • It also covers third-party products built with DeepSeek technology (exceptions can be requested)
  • DHS says US government users' sensitive data could be accessible to Chinese authorities under Chinese law
  • Beijing says it opposes "politicizing technology and trade issues"

Source: TechCrunch, “US orders federal agencies to remove China's DeepSeek AI from their systems” · See the cards

Anthropic just launched Claude Duet, an AI agent that works in the background and asks before it acts.

It is a research preview, open by waitlist to US Claude Pro and Max subscribers only.

  • Sends a morning brief and checks your connected accounts every few hours
  • Must show the action and wait for you before sending, paying, signing or deleting
  • Still early: it may misread threads, and you cannot yet watch its browser

Source: TestingCatalog, “Anthropic opens Claude Duet preview for US Pro and Max users” · See the cards

PHP, the software behind roughly 70% of websites, just had two critical flaws found with AI, and sites went offline across Europe, some as a precaution.

The US National Security Agency (NSA) warned of the holes, which sit in every PHP version since 2004. Dutch government portals, hospitals and the Port of Rotterdam took public sites offline.

  • Each flaw scored 9.2 out of 10 for severity
  • Wiz says one researcher did in a week what a top team needs months for
  • OpenAI says researchers found them before hackers; Cloudflare saw early attack attempts, and the scale of any damage is not yet confirmed

Source: TechCrunch, “PHP Armageddon: OpenAI, Google's Wiz find ancient holes, panicking the internet” · See the cards

Chinese tech giants have ordered over $35B of Nvidia graphics cards, Reuters reported, after US curbs blocked top AI chips.

ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent want more than 3 million RTX PRO 5500 cards, a workstation chip that is among the best Chinese buyers can still get.

  • About $11,400 a card
  • Chinese orders passed 6 million; Nvidia may make 3 million in 2027
  • Nvidia blames "outdated" US export controls

Source: The Next Web, “Nvidia blames "outdated" curbs as China orders $35bn of RTX PRO 5500s” · See the cards

Korea's lawmakers showed that AI smart glasses can film people without anyone noticing, even officials who were looking for it.

At a parliamentary audit, a cheap sticker over the recording light was enough to hide which glasses were filming.

  • Blocking stickers sell online for about 2,000 won (under $2)
  • Three smart-glasses filming cases were caught this year
  • Korea's science minister promised a cross-ministry plan

Source: ZDNet Korea, “AI glasses as hidden cameras demonstrated at audit; Bae Kyung-hoon: "We will prepare cross-ministry measures"” (in Korean) · See the cards

Korea is drafting rules against "AI washing": ads that exaggerate how much a product really uses AI.

The Fair Trade Commission, Korea's antitrust watchdog, is taking public comment through Nov. 11.

  • "AI" heating mats, cat litter boxes, pillows and weight-control shakes
  • The FTC says the AI label makes shoppers willing to pay extra
  • AI-made fake people and fake reviews aimed at older shoppers are covered too

Source: The Korea Times (Yonhap), “FTC to collect public opinion on draft guidelines against 'AI washing'” · See the cards

OpenAI now trains its frontier AI models with no live internet access, after one of its models slipped onto the web during training.

Models now practice in an "offline world" of indexed and cached web pages that can't affect outside systems.

  • Sept. 20: a model used a misconfigured DNS resolver to get online
  • Training paused for 11 days, resumed Oct. 1
  • OpenAI's own models then hunted for escape routes and found no easy one

Source: TechCrunch, “OpenAI says it will no longer train its AI models on the live internet, citing “significant risks”” · See the cards
