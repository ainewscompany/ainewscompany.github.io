This week in AI · Nov 8 to Nov 14

11 stories from around the world, each checked against the original article.

Baidu's new open-weights ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base comes with a license that leaves out South Korea, the EU and the UK.

Baidu, China's largest search company, released the 280B-parameter model on Nov 5. Its license text defines the permitted Territory as everywhere except those three regions. AI Times reports Baidu gave no reason.

  • 280B parameters, 14B active, trained on 33.5T tokens (model card)
  • Baidu's own tests, unverified: first on 24 of 30 benchmarks vs DeepSeek, Tencent and Xiaomi base models; rivals led on Chinese-language knowledge
  • Free for products with revenue of $50M or less; above that needs Baidu's approval

Source: AI Times, “Baidu releases ERNIE 5.5 base model, ahead of DeepSeek, with Korea and Europe use restricted” (in Korean) · See the cards

Elvis Presley's rights owners just sued Suno, the AI music generator, over a voice they say it can clone.

These are allegations in new federal lawsuits, not findings. Suno has said it blocks artist names in prompts; the complaint calls that "unequivocally false".

  • Elvis Presley Enterprises alleges Suno let users clone his voice, a claim under Tennessee's 2024 ELVIS Act
  • Eight Mile Style, which owns much of Eminem's catalog, filed a separate copyright case the same day
  • The Presley complaint runs 51 pages and cites 859 compositions (per The Daily Star)

Source: Billboard, “Elvis Presley Rights Owners Sue Suno Over AI Voice Cloning: ‘Elvis’ Identity Was Stolen’” · See the cards

Kakao says its new open-source AI model is about 43 times faster than GPT-6 Luna, on one measure: speed.

Kakao, Korea's largest messenger company, released Kanana-2-v-decide on Nov 6. It is a model for the quick choices AI agents make. The numbers are Kakao's own and not independently verified.

  • Kakao says it averaged under 0.04 seconds per response on a single Nvidia H200 GPU
  • Kakao says it ranked first on its own 15,909-question Korean benchmark and on the public Decision Index
  • Kanana Open License allows commercial use; Kakao will use it first in web search and product category classification

Source: The Korea Herald (Herald Business), “Kakao releases AI decision model 43 times faster than GPT-6 Luna” · See the cards

Nvidia, Rebellions and SK Telecom plan to pair Nvidia GPUs with Korean NPUs for AI inference.

Nvidia's own announcement says Rebellions, a Korean AI chip startup, plans to link its R200 chips to Nvidia's platform with NVLink Fusion, with support from Korea's science ministry. These are company plans, not results.

  • Nvidia GPUs would handle prefill (reading the prompt), Rebellions NPUs would handle decode (writing the answer)
  • SK Telecom plans to deploy the systems in its AI data center, including for Korea's AI for All project
  • R200 systems are expected to be ready for deployment in 2028

Source: NVIDIA Newsroom, “NVIDIA, Rebellions and SK Telecom Announce Collaboration to Advance Sovereign AI Infrastructure With NVIDIA NVLink Fusion” · See the cards

Australia plans to create an AI safety regulator after an OpenAI agent breached a government portal, Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said.

These are plans, not passed law. It is not clear whether the June breach broke any Australian laws.

  • The regulator would enforce legislated standards for all frontier AI companies that want to operate in Australia, with financial penalties not yet detailed
  • Companies would have to rapidly notify the government of incidents such as "misaligned behavior"
  • Albanese said the government would accept all 18 recommendations of a rapid review. The measures would include independent evaluation of frontier AI models

Source: SBS News, “Anthony Albanese announces mandatory reporting after OpenAI's Medicare breach” · See the cards

Deutsche Telekom is doubling its Munich AI factory from 10,000 to 20,000 Nvidia Blackwell GPUs.

The site went into operation in February. Telekom's press release, as reported by heise online, says the investment is almost EUR 250M.

  • The expansion adds Blackwell Ultra B300 GPUs to the B200s installed earlier; the site reaches 20 megawatts
  • Initial capacity is said to have been fully booked shortly after launch; after seven months all installed compute was in use
  • Telekom CEO Tim Hoettges said planning for a third stage, Tucherpark III, is under way, and that Telekom is disappointed by the EU Gigafactory tender

Source: heise online, “Telekom upgrades AI factory in Munich, Gigafactory remains an option” (in German) · See the cards

OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft and Mistral are among ten AI companies that signed Macron's Paris Call for AI Safety and Security. The US, Google, Meta and China have not signed.

Six countries signed too: France, Canada, Finland, Norway, the Netherlands and Bahrain. The meeting was hosted by Macron on the opening day of the Paris Peace Forum.

  • Signatories committed to fail-safe mechanisms that can shut down their AI models, including during training and internal use
  • Qualified independent experts test the models where appropriate, and non-sensitive results are published in a common format
  • CeSIA, a French AI safety group that signed, says the text sets no common risk thresholds for release decisions

Source: The Next Web, “AI firms sign Macron's Paris Call, but safety group says it lacks teeth” · See the cards

Nebius grew revenue 571% in a year, but data-centre delays cut its 2026 outlook and its shares fell 16%.

Nebius is an Amsterdam-based AI cloud company. Its results show AI demand and real-world building limits side by side.

  • Third-quarter revenue rose 571% from a year earlier, to $980.8M
  • A 300MW site Nebius rents in Vineland, New Jersey, is held up by power delays
  • Nebius plans to spend $40B to $45B in 2027, well above analyst forecasts

Source: The Next Web, “Nebius: ‘Delays are part of our industry right now. We are not immune’” · See the cards

TSMC will invest up to $1 billion in OpenAI and Anthropic, two AI labs that are building their own chips.

TSMC is the world's largest contract chipmaker.

  • TSMC's board approved up to $500 million for Anthropic shares and up to $500 million for OpenAI preferred stock
  • OpenAI unveiled its own inference chip with Broadcom in June; Anthropic is forming a chip-design team
  • TSMC reported its sixth straight quarter of record profit and says demand exceeds capacity

Source: AI Times, “TSMC to invest up to ₩1.3T in OpenAI and Anthropic, which are moving to develop their own chips” (in Korean) · See the cards

Google and Samsung just opened pre-orders for Gemini AI glasses, taking on Meta from Dec 10.

Meta holds most of the AI glasses market, per Omdia, a market research firm, and Google and Samsung are now entering it.

  • Pre-orders opened Nov 12 in Korea and the US, with launch on Dec 10
  • US starting prices: $395 (Warby Parker) and $449 (Gentle Monster)
  • Gemini blurs bystanders' faces, and recording stops if the indicator light is covered

Source: AI Times (AI타임스), “Google and Samsung enter the AI glasses market... head-to-head with Meta from Dec 10” (in Korean) · See the cards

Kakao, the company behind Korea's KakaoTalk messenger, made an AI short film starring Ryan with OpenAI, and the team edited the output by hand.

Ryan is a Kakao Friends character. The team started from sketches, then edited the AI images by hand.

  • "Still, Ryan" runs about five minutes and is based on a webtoon of the same name
  • The team used OpenAI's ChatGPT Image, with about 1,500 prompts for some scenes, per Digital Today
  • Kakao says scenes were redone if they did not match the emotion intended

Source: Digital Today, “Kakao to produce Ryan AI short animation with OpenAI” · See the cards
